> *Ten years at Microsoft, and I marked the milestone the same way I've spent much of the decade: onsite with a customer, helping people learn something new.*

---

Ten years ago this week, I joined Microsoft.

I celebrated the anniversary in Cincinnati, Ohio, delivering Azure training for a customer. There was no office party or carefully planned victory lap—just a hotel, a classroom, and another opportunity to learn alongside the people I was there to teach.

Somehow, that felt exactly right.

In **[My First Year at Microsoft](/posts/my-first-year-at-microsoft/)**, I wrote about discovering that expertise wasn't about having every answer. It was about knowing how to find the right people and ask better questions.

Ten years later, I still believe that. The difference is that I now spend more of my time helping other people find their own answers.

<!--

The longer I do this work, the less a career feels like a collection of answers. It feels more like a long conversation—with customers, colleagues, technology, and earlier versions of myself.

That conversation was still going in a classroom in Cincinnati.

## Looking Ahead

<!--

One year in, I was beginning to feel like I belonged.

Five years in, I was learning how experience changes the questions.

Ten years in, I am grateful that there are still new questions to ask—and new people to ask them with.
